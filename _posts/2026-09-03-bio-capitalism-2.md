Now I reread **Life put to work** with the most charitable perspective possible.  I cannot admit that the foundation of this theory is at all sensible, but within this paper is a massive expansion of what counts as work, meaning that if we remain within capitalism, this is work that ought to be receive compensation.  

A calculation could be made for the cost of relational labour, linguistic and cognitive labour, symbolic labour, corporeal and sensorial labour, and affective labour. (240)  For example, reparations for the Anishinaabe who were forced or instructed to attend boarding school, where they forgot Ojibwe and learned English, justifyable based ultimately on the total cost to the *"general intellect"*.  

Most positively, this has allowed for the creation of new hyper-specific caretaker jobs like the *death doula*, emotionally-motivated writers like myself, and even the borderline-schizophrenic who zeroes in on the Flock camera as the source of all western suffering.  Even they should be paid for their labour in participating in grassroots anarchist communities.  

Of course though, this hypothetical would be the nail in the coffin for any anticapitalist knowledge.  Say we collectively forgot that money is fiat currency, an abstract concept that only functions when both parties believe that it functions.  This is already happening with how the internalization of ideas like autism and borderline personality disorder cause the individual harm, which then is compensated for as disability pay.  Should an individual lose faith in the value of money, the compensation loses the ability to function as a pacifier.  The anticapitalist would be forever unsatisfied with a life as a consumer of media, a completer of captchas, a trainer of LLMs and reccomendation algorithms, a writer on wikipedia, or a body at a concert.  If capitalism can provide a decent enough living situation, to some, it would be worth the cost of true freedom.  Still, there will always be some who perfer poverty with freedom-anguish and death to complacency and the mantling of neuro-divergence.  The solution is there in the scope of the *general intellect*, as if one opts out, the suffering they may or may not be experiencing cannot possibly be known to the biocapitalists during the calculation of who deserves how much.  To the biocapitalist, there is actually no such thing as rivalling ideas on the outside, they believe anarchist thought to be monetizable, documentable opinions or trends in human behaviour- anthropology.  

**The cost of opting out of biocapitalism simply cannot be paid.**

The biocapitalist is also "supremely interested in *differences*.  Difference, even under the apperance of knowledge of indigenous or local systems, has become a highly valued commodity to be spent on the market" (239).

Diversity is our strength, yes.  Simultaneously, ethnic or cognative differences as points of commodification is dangerous.  There is potential to create books, educational courses, ticketed events, etc. that bridge these differences to share knowledge.  There is potential also to turn the off-the-grid anarchists into celebrities, artists, content creators, or globalist speakers.  Only, to do any of that risks the erasure of knowledge that exists in pre-language minds or extrasocial environments.  It also requires the creation of an industry by the biocapitalists that rewards ideas that threaten biocapitalism.  Though digital communication tools like Instagram, Discord, and YouTube could do this, users who spread threatening ideas are routinely banned, shadow-banned, or demonitized by the police bot.  If the police bot does not exist, we have the early days of 4chan- facilitating the exploitation of minors and criminal organizations, which if we adhere faithfully to the biocapistalist theory, the work that goes into these practices are also of interest.  The strict biocapitalist would pay money for the answers to questions such as "how does one shut off their moral sense so as to engage in human trafficing?", "Are Steve Bannon and Jeffery Epstein biocapitalists?", "Are we the bad guys?"  

Unfortunately, a balance would have to be weighed: the cost of personal guilt and the labour of reconciliation against the knowledge that biocapitalism itself is not in and of itself a complete framework for reality.  The acceptance that we were wrong, and we may have to give up more than we expected in the pursuit of knowledge.  For this reason biocapitalism is a momentary idea in the developing intelligence of life that will either be abandoned in the pursuit of knowledge, or walled off into a less-encompacing form like capitalism, authoritarianism, nationalism, et cetera.
